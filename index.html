How the idea changed

Four years, one mechanism.

2022
Liquity Chicken Bonds

You deposit LUSD. If you leave early, you get the whole deposit back and give up the balance you had accrued. They call it chickening out.

(LUSD Chicken Bonds docs, live October 2022)
2023
Bear Bonds

You deposit ETH. It becomes stETH. If you leave early, you get the ETH back. The yield goes to open bonds.

Built in about 8 hours by 0xbeans and friends at ETHOnline 2023. Three sponsor prizes and a finalist spot. He said that if enough people liked it, he would clean up the code and deploy it on mainnet.
(ETHGlobal showcase) (his thread, Nov 2023)
THEN
The printer problem

If a token keeps printing after people sell, the people who stay get diluted. That makes more people sell.

2026
Standard Reserve

One ETH pool. If more ETH comes in, issuance can rise. If ETH leaves, issuance drops and the system buys tokens back. A charter is a license. Each branch gets a share of new tokens. Cash out a branch to take tokens. Cash out the last branch and the charter burns.

(whitepaper v0.1)

one of my favorite mechanisms is making exits reward the people who believe

back in 2023 i won an ETHGlobal hackathon with bear bonds, a system that redirected value from sellers to holders

we took that idea, rebuilt it from the ground up, and supercharged it

(0xbeans on X, 23 Aug 2026)

The mechanics, as the paper prints them

Transcribed from whitepaper v0.1, symbol by symbol. Where the paper blacks a value out, this page blacks it out too, at the same width. From the paper means the formula is printed there with its numbers. Shape only means the structure is printed but the values inside it are not. Blacked out means nothing survives. Nothing here is reconstructed or guessed.

The signal
signal(n) = F(n−1) + F(n−2)
F(n) = −
The signal is printed exactly as you see it. F(n) is the net ETH flow for one epoch, everything that came in on buys minus everything that left on sells, and both halves of it are blacked out. Issuance reads the last two completed epochs, so one loud hour cannot swing it.

The two regimes
F(n) > 0 → expansion    F(n) ≤ 0 → contraction
Two levers, two clocks. Where the fees go is decided by the epoch happening now, F(n), so defence reacts fast. How much is issued moves on the slower signal above. Note where zero sits: flat is not neutral, it counts as contraction. The paper puts the asymmetry plainly: cuts are immediate, raises must be earned. Stated in prose, not typeset as a formula.

d is the epoch length in days and m is the policy multiplier. Only the base rate is blacked out, so the structure is the paper's, not ours.

What one branch earns
one branch per day = × m / N
This is the paper's own formula for a single branch's daily yield, where N is every branch in the system. So your share is simply your branches over N. Issuance streams second by second, and a new branch earns from the moment it opens. One to ten branches per charter, and every new one costs $STANDARD that is burned.
shape only
Exit pressure
P = W / max(D + W, )
fee =
W is everything withdrawn across the system in the last 7 days, D is everything still held. So the fee reads how much left, not how many wallets left. The pressure formula is printed; the fee built on top of it is one long bar. The paper does say its shape: a quadratic from a blacked out floor to a blacked out ceiling, half of it burned, half paid to the people who stayed.
shape only
Buyback limit
spend per tick = min(0.10 × V, 0.002 × R)
The only formula in the paper that still has live numbers in it. In contraction the vault buys $STANDARD and burns it, once an hour, where V is the vault balance and R is the pool reserves. That caps buying near 5% of pool depth a day at launch settings, so the defence cannot be baited into one blockable shot. Unspent balance rolls forward and the vault can never sell.

Supply
S(t) = 100,000,000 + M(t) − B(t)
Smax(t) = 1,000,000,000 − B(t)
Both printed with their numbers intact. Genesis liquidity, plus everything ever minted when a banker withdrew, minus everything ever burned. Burned tokens do not come back, so the ceiling can only fall.
from the paper
Going silent
bounty = min(0.02 × balance, 100,000)
revocation = 0.70 × balance    returned = 0.30 × balance
The paper states these as numbered steps rather than as a formula; the arithmetic here is just its own numbers written out. After 30 days of silence anyone can report the wallet. Half the revocation burns, half pays the bankers still working. It also bounds one hidden number on purpose: it calls this 70% deliberately worse than the worst case exit fee, so the exit ceiling sits below 70% without ever being printed.
